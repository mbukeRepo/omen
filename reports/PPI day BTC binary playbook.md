## What history says: confirmation continues, contradiction converts

There is no formal event study of BTC around PPI — the best measured work ([Block Scholes](https://www.blockscholes.com/institutional-research/is-bitcoin-showing-greater-sensitivity-to-us-cpi-releases-again)) covers CPI only, finding that in 2026 **CPI surprises explain ~80% of BTC's first-hour return on a rolling 12-release window, with the effect concentrated in the first hour and gone by 4–24h**. The practical translation: the directional information in today's print will be fully expressed within ~60 minutes; after that, flows (the 13:30 UTC equity open, end-of-day ETF prints) take over. Anecdotally, large PPI surprises (3–4x consensus, like Aug 2025's +0.9% vs +0.2%) have produced CPI-sized BTC moves of **−2.5% to −3%** ([Yahoo Finance/Decrypt](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bitcoin-ethereum-fall-ppi-shock-190003159.html)), while a modest beat alongside firm retail sales moved BTC under 1% (Mar 2024 — [Pintu/Coingape](https://pintu.co.id/en/news/76299-inflasi-ppi-as-februari-bitcoin-anjlok)).

The day-after-hot-CPI setup has a specific case record, and it is not friendly to YES. **When PPI confirms the prior day's hot CPI, continuation through support has been the pattern**: May 2026 (hot CPI May 13, hot PPI May 14) broke BTC below $80,000 on day three of the selloff ([syndicated coverage](https://www.mexc.com/news/596567), approximate); Feb 2026's hot PPI extended an existing downtrend another 2.5–3% ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-threatens-new-breakdown-us-ppi-gold-1-month-high)). When PPI *contradicts* the setup, the bounce has convexity because it removes a freshly priced tail — the Jan 2023 both-soft print spiked BTC to a 4-month high ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-sees-new-4-month-high-as-us-ppi-retail-data-posts-big-misses)) — and with October hike odds at 35–45%, there is a real tail to remove today.

Two more patterns bound the playbook. First, the **US equity open at 13:30 UTC is the recurring reversal timestamp**: post-print spikes got sold at the open in Jul 2025, Jan 2023, and the Mar/Sep 2024 CPI whipsaws — so the first hour's direction should be trusted, the second hour's extension should not. Second, mean reversion is real but shallow: hour-scale BTC returns show significant negative autocorrelation ([Ledger journal](https://ledger.pitt.edu/ojs/ledger/article/view/213)), and small-sample crash studies find a positive median next-day bounce **but a ~92% probability the crash low is revisited within 30 days** ([Algocrat](https://t.me/s/algocrat/151), vendor-grade) — any post-PPI bounce is a rent, not a regime change. Finally, when PPI and retail sales conflict, the first 30 minutes whipsaws and the initial direction is often wrong — size down or wait for the open.

## The playbook: scenario-by-scenario actions for the 80,555 binary

**Pricing check first.** YES at 0.70 implies P(BTC ≥ 80,555 at 06:00 UTC Oct 16) ≈ 70%. Weighting the scenarios below (hot ~25–30%, in-line ~45–50%, soft ~25%) against their conditional settle-above-strike odds gives a true P(YES) estimate of roughly 0.62–0.67 — slightly below the 0.70 ask. The pre-print edge is thin and leans NO; the real edge is reacting faster than the binary's book reprices after the number.

**Now, pre-12:30 UTC:** Do not buy YES at 0.70. If you hold YES from lower, trim into 0.70 — you are being paid roughly fair value for a coin you're about to flip. The defensible pre-print position is small NO at ≤0.30 (risking 0.30 to win 0.70 on scenarios — hot print, in-line-then-fade, or a Warsh hawkish surprise at 03:30 UTC — whose combined probability plausibly exceeds 30%), sized as a tail position you can afford to lose entirely. ## Conclusion

The subtle edge in this market is structural, not directional: the binary at 0.70 prices pure diffusion, but today's distribution is not diffuse — it is trimodal around a data print whose signal lives in one sub-index (core ex-trade) that the headline will actively obscure for the first few seconds. Whoever parses the core line before the binary's book reprices has the trade; the headline will likely print "hot" (0.6–0.7%) and mean nothing. The second structural point: every technical target on the downside map ($80.0k, $79.7k, $78.2k, $78k max pain) is below the strike while the upside needs no travel at all — so NO is the only side with destinations, and the scenario-weighted fair value of YES (~0.62–0.67) sits below the market. That gap is real but thin; it funds a small pre-print NO, not a conviction position.

The larger lesson this setup teaches: in a hiking-cycle regime where BTC trades as Nasdaq-chip beta, the day-two inflation print after a hot CPI is a referendum on the rate path, and its convexity is one-sided — confirmation extends a move that is already partly priced, while contradiction kills a freshly priced tail. Today that asymmetry favors the soft-print scenario in spot terms (bigger move) but the hot-print scenario in binary terms (bigger payout per unit of premium). The playbook above resolves that tension the only way an event trader can: stay near flat into the number, and be the fastest size in the book for sixty minutes after it.
